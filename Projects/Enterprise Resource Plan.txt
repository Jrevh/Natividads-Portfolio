Enterprise Resource Plan (ERP)
1. Executive Summary
Project Title: Happy Pixies Event Management System
Organization/Department:
Prepared By: Group 3
Date: May 21, 2025
Purpose: The ERP plan is designed to consolidate and streamline Happy Pixies' core business processes, a
developing event management firm. The deployment of the ERP system will make service delivery more
efficient, help in optimizing resources, and enable real-time decisions within departments like inventory,
scheduling, finance, and logistics.
2. Project Objectives
This enterprise resource plan is designed to develop a digital event management system and aims to:
● Increase operational efficiency
● Integrate core business processes under a single system
● Improve real-time reporting and business decision-making
● Enhance data accuracy, accessibility, and security
● Enable mobile-friendly and offline access for on-site events
● Support staff scheduling and vendor coordination
● Ensure financial accountability via invoicing and payment tracking
3. Scope of the ERP System
Inclusions:
● Client Booking and CRM
● Staff Scheduling and HR
● Inventory and Warehouse Management
● Finance: Invoicing, Budgeting, and Payments
● Procurement and Vendor Coordination
● Event Execution and Logistics Management
Exclusions:
● Full-scale e-commerce features (product selling)
● Advanced AI-based event planning automation
Geographic/Organizational Scope:
● Head Office with support for mobile field operations during on-site events
4. Current System Assessment
Existing Systems:
● Unused inventory tracking system
● Manual contracts and invoices
● Social media and messaging apps for bookings
Pain Points:
● Double-entry errors
● Lack of real-time inventory updates
● Manual scheduling inefficiencies
Integration Challenges:
● Revamping the existing inventory system and merging with database-driven modules
● Staff adaptation to digital workflow
User Feedback Summary:
● For customers: Desire for faster booking management, especially in Social Media
● Need for accessible records during events
● Request for auto-generated documents

10. Change Management Plan
Communication Strategy
● Primary Channels:
○ Group Chat: A dedicated group chat via Facebook Messenger will be used for quick
updates and coordination.
○ Notice Boards: Printed updates and step-by-step guides will be posted on the office
bulletin board or shared via email for easy reference.
○ Weekly Check-Ins: A short team meeting (15–20 minutes) every week to share progress,
address questions, and gather feedback.
● Documentation:
Printed manuals and simple digital guides (PDF format) will be shared via Google Drive or
printed for office use.
Stakeholder Engagement
● Team Inclusion:
Employees will be involved early in the process through discussions and brainstorming sessions
to raise concerns, suggest improvements, and understand how the ERP will help their daily tasks.
● Feedback Collection:
Feedback will be collected informally through one-on-one conversations, suggestion boxes, or a
short monthly Google Form.
Internal Champions:
Select 1–2 employees with strong familiarity in business operations will be trained ahead of
others and act as "go-to" people during and after implementation.
Training Programs
● On-Site Training Sessions:
Brief training sessions (30–60 minutes each) conducted in small groups or individually
depending on employee roles.
● Quick Reference Guides:
Each user will receive a printed cheat sheet or laminated card with common ERP tasks and steps
(e.g., “How to record a sale” or “How to check inventory”).
● Follow-up Support:
For the first 2–3 weeks after go-live, employees can get one-on-one help from the internal
champion or project manager for troubleshooting.
Resistance Management
● Personalized Orientation:
Before training, a casual team talk will be held to explain why the ERP is needed (e.g., to reduce
paperwork, avoid errors, save time). This sets the tone for openness.
● Gradual Introduction:
New features will be introduced one at a time to avoid overwhelming the team. For example,
start with inventory tracking, then later add sales reporting.
● Encouragement and Patience:
Management will maintain an open-door policy for concerns. Employees will be reminded that
mistakes are okay during the learning period.
● Recognition:
Employees who actively help others or learn quickly will be recognized during team
meetings—simple gestures like snacks, thank-you notes, or verbal appreciation go a long way.

12. Evaluation Metrics
To quantify the effect of installing an integrated business management system on an event planning
business, an official Key Performance Indicators (KPI) plan will be created. The KPIs will also be in line
with the business priorities and strategic goals of the company, providing measurable indicators to track
system performance, efficiency, and quality of service improvements.
To gauge operational effectiveness, the following KPIs are planned to be used:
● Average planning time per event
● Number of events attended per employee per month
● Reduction in percentage of human coordination activities (i.e., emails, calls)
These measurements will serve to show how automation impacts administrative workload and
throughput among planning and execution teams.
In order to assess the level of system integration among core business processes, the research will
measure:
● Number of processes centralized (e.g., booking, invoicing, vendor management)
● Percentage of active users using the system on a regular basis
● Time gained through fewer tool switches between platforms
These KPIs will help gauge if the company is running more efficiently with an integrated software
environment. Besides making real-time reporting and decision-making possible, the following KPIs will
also be monitored:
● Average time to produce financial or operating reports
● Decision-makers' use of dashboard frequency
● System-generated information-based ratio of decisions
● Client feedback response time
These measures will illustrate ways in which data visibility and accessibility promote timely,
knowledge-based decision-making at both operational and management levels.
In order to ensure data accuracy, accessibility, and security, the company will track:
● Booking error rate (duplicate or incorrect bookings)
● % of client/vendor files stored safely in electronic form
● Mean time to get event-related information
● Incident or data breach rate related to access
These controls will keep information assets up to date and protected, enabling timely execution and
compliance.
Considering the mobile and location-based characteristics of event operations, the following
mobile-supportive and offline-enabled KPIs will be considered:
● Percentage of work performed on-site utilizing mobile devices
● Offline sync success rate
● Employee-provided mobile usability scores
● Frequency breakdown of mobile equipment usage at events
These measurements will be leveraged to enable team agility and support real-time field operations,
even in low-connectivity scenarios. To record staff and vendor coordination, the system will capture:
● Shift confirmation and fill rates
● Mean monthly rate of scheduling conflict or revision
● Average vendor response time to inquiries
● Staff no-show rate by event
These KPIs will be instrumental in maintaining service reliability and operational readiness, especially
during high-pressure hours. Lastly, to ensure fiscal responsibility by payment and billing control, the
system will track:
● Invoice accuracy rate
● Event finalization-invoicing lag time
● Ratio of payments accepted within 14 or 30 days
● Amount of outstanding receivables
These financial factors will assist the company in ensuring good cash flow, reducing billing discrepancies,
and enhancing collection effectiveness.
